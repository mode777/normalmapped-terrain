# Tiling detail normal: spike findings

Written November 2026 after the `add-tiling-detail-normal` spike. The QA
screenshots referenced below were captured with chrome-headless-shell +
SwiftShader on the SSH verification host; they are local output, not checked
in. The driver writes them to `shots-infinite/` and `shots-dem/` (the copies
reviewed here are under `shots-add-tiling-detail/`).

## Verdict

The tiling detail normal works as a **heightfield detail layer**:

- No vertex tangents and no TBN are needed. The base and detail normals are
  height gradients in the same world-x/z frame; composing them in slope space
  keeps the base slope on steep terrain and preserves the existing base look
  exactly at detail strength 0.
- The detail is world-locked: a 25 km warp away and back changed 0.033 % of
  pixels on the infinite page and 0.005 % on the DEM page (the remaining
  differences are UI/tile-loading edges, not a pattern shift).
- The object-space tile orientation is correct: the low-sun east/west pair
  moves the highlights to the sun-facing side, with no inverted bumps or pits.
- The QA loops pass on both pages with no console or page errors. The base
  normal reference values are unchanged (`hf=9.17` vs `0.23` on the infinite
  page; `2.25` vs `0.36` on the DEM page), which also confirms the slope-space
  base refactor did not change the existing render.

The remaining decision is projection, not tangents: planar `x/z` is exact for
a heightfield detail but stretches on cliffs. That is acceptable for the
current "fine relief on the terrain" use. If the material becomes a
**surface coating** with uniform texel density on cliff faces, the follow-up is
triplanar or a per-fragment surface frame.

## What was tested

- `public/materials/detail-diagnostic-normal.png`: 512² RGBA8 object-space
  normals (`R=N.x`, `G=N.z`, `B=N.y`), generated by `npm run bake:detail`.
  Reference tile: 16 m world size, 4 m relief for normalized height 1. The
  shader's detail strength scales the decoded slope; the 4 m relief is not the
  final visual amplitude.
- Infinite page: 16 m tile, fade 80–250 m.
- DEM page: 32 m tile, fade 150–500 m.
- QA detail strength: **0.35**. A probe at 0, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5 and 0.7 showed
  that 0.35 is a clear diagnostic value but already strong, especially on the
  infinite page's fine 16 m tile.
- Shots: `09-detail-closeup-off` / `10-detail-closeup-on` (analyzer pair),
  `11-detail-sun-east` / `12-detail-sun-west` (20° sun), `13-detail-cliff`,
  `14-detail-warp-before` / `15-detail-warp-after`. The base-on/detail-off and
  detail-on default-pose shots are `02b` / `02c`; `02-normal0` zeroes both
  strengths and is still a valid flat baseline.

## Results

| Check | Infinite | DEM |
|---|---|---|
| detail-off close-up `hf` | 1.15 | 0.80 |
| detail-on close-up `hf` | 11.59 | 10.39 |
| detail-on / detail-off | 10.1× | 13.0× |
| warp round-trip pixel diff | 0.033 % | 0.005 % |
| draw calls | 4 | 4 |
| triangles | 165k | 102k |

The layer adds one texture fetch and about 1.4 MiB of RGBA8 texture with mips
(plus the compressed PNG), and no draw calls. The software-renderer FPS in the
shots is not a performance measurement; a real-GPU frame-time check is still
outstanding.

### Orientation and tiling

The diagnostic tile has an anisotropic diagonal and a ridged layer, so a
green-channel flip or an axis swap is visible. The east/west pair is lit
consistently with the base terrain, and the detail stays attached to the
surface in the auto-cruise and warp shots.

A single 16 m / 32 m tile is still a single repeat. The distance fade keeps the
near-field readable, but there is no multi-scale or rotated sampling in this
spike. If the repetition becomes objectionable in production, the next step is
two or three rotated scales composed in slope space (the rotation is applied
to the decoded slope as well as the UV).

### Clipping and cliffs

The close-ups and the 500 km warp show no per-ring seam, no swim and no rebase
pop; the detail UV is derived from the interpolated `vWorldPos.xz`, not from
ring-local positions. The cliff shot shows the expected planar stretch on a
steep face. It reads as a heightfield detail, not as a uniform rock coating;
this is the evidence for choosing planar now and triplanar only if the material
goal changes.

### Filtering and aliasing

The tile uses `RepeatWrapping`, mipmaps, trilinear filtering and
`anisotropy = min(8, max)`. No obvious sparkle or grid is visible in the
static shots, but SwiftShader is not representative for anisotropic filtering
or specular sparkle. A real-GPU pass at low altitude and a moving camera is
required before production sign-off.

## Recommendation

1. Keep the spike's planar `x/z`, slope-space detail layer for a heightfield
   detail. It is exact for the terrain's parameterization, cheap and stable.
2. Ship with the detail **off by default** until the real-GPU pass and an art
   review. The slider should expose a useful range; the diagnostic tile at
   strength 0.35 is already strong.
3. Recommended starting values for a production material:
   - infinite: 16 m tile, strength 0.20–0.30, fade 80–250 m;
   - DEM: 24–32 m tile, strength 0.25–0.35, fade 150–500 m.
   The DEM's 32 m tile reads as broad ripples at close range; a 16–24 m tile is
   worth testing with the real material art.
4. Add two rotated scales (for example 1× and 0.37×) if repetition is
   visible; stay in slope space and rotate the decoded slope with the UV.
5. If the material becomes a surface coating on cliffs, add triplanar as a
   separate change. Do not add per-vertex tangents; the frame is derivable and
   the existing object-space convention is the reason the atlas is robust.
6. The detail is shading-only. Silhouette, collision and parallax occlusion
   mapping are out of scope.

## Limitations

- The diagnostic tile is not final art; it is deliberately strong and
  asymmetric to catch orientation errors.
- The QA host uses software WebGL. The stills prove the pipeline, not the
  real-GPU filter quality or specular stability.
- No triplanar, multi-scale, stochastic tiling or POM was implemented.
- The detail strength is scale-free; changing a tile's world size changes the
  apparent bumpiness unless the strength is compensated.
